# TSWS shot prompts

How to turn one TSWS shot into prompts that a generator can run and a reviewer can check, without inventing canon. The canon itself is not in this file. It lives on Drive and changes by dated ruling, so every run starts by reading it. Where this file restates a ruling so that a still can be checked against it, it names that ruling and its date, and any newer ruling on Drive overrides the restatement. This file holds the method and what was measured the first time it ran. `references/SH020_job_2026-10-30.md` is the record of that first run: the prompts as submitted, the prediction ids, the hashes and the prices.

For SH020, SH070, SH100 and SH150 also load `tsws-repair-workflows`. It holds those shots' repair history and the measurements behind them, SH150's pan among them. It was compiled on 2026-10-13, before the mask ruling of 2026-10-25, so its shell wording describes the old footage and the superseded design. Where it names a face-shaped shell or a shell mesh as something to build, read the Venetian half-mask of the v1.4 sheet instead, and take no mask design from it. Its boundary of no paid generation governs its repair methods; a paid generation under this skill is Tee's budget call, asked on a card (see the "Spend only on Tee's answer" bullet below).

Terms. The five Episode 1 street shots are SH020, SH070, SH100, SH135 and SH150, on one locked street camera. "The fall" is the moment in SH100 when the street crowd's masks detach. The maroon-scarf pedestrian (dark coat, maroon scarf, black shoulder bag) is the woman the 2026-10-16 street proposal named as SH070's stopper; G01, the grey-coat woman with the black handbag, is the stopper the 2026-10-25 ruling names; Ruling 2 of 2026-10-30 confirmed her over the proposal.

## The method

Steps 6 to 8 were worked out on the chained Episode 1 street shots, where one shot's end still is the next shot's start frame. For any other shot, say which of them apply and why the rest do not.

1. **Pick the shot from the record, not from memory.** The manifest names each shot's editor verdict and logged defects. Start there and quote the exact cue.
2. **Get the native master and verify it.** The "Getting media in" bullet of `tsws-repair-workflows` names the editor's acceptance list that holds each master's sha256 and the folder that holds the street masters. The Drive connector refuses downloads over 10 MB. A larger master downloads only while it is link-shared, which Tee switches by hand in the Drive app. Check the sha256 against the editor's record. Afterwards, ask Tee to set it back to Restricted, then read back both its permissions and an unsigned download. On 2026-10-30 the SH020 and SH150 masters were found still open to anyone with the link, left that way since 2026-10-16. Tee set both to Restricted the same day, and the read back showed only the owner on each and a sign-in page for an unsigned download (Ruling 3 of 2026-10-30).
3. **Look at the frames person by person.** Count the frames with `ffprobe -v error -count_frames -select_streams v:0 -show_entries stream=nb_read_frames -of csv=p=0 MASTER.mp4`, then take each end at native size with `ffmpeg -i MASTER.mp4 -vf "select=eq(n\,N)" -fps_mode passthrough -frames:v 1 fN.png`, N being 0 and the count minus one, plus a contact sheet across the take. On SH020 this reproduced the saved native frames exactly. Write a table of every visible person: what they carry at each end and what they need. Note who leaves or enters the frame. Then read the table against the manifest's Logged Defects. For SH020 the editor had logged costume-like masks, an unmasked beige-coat face and a hair and shell discontinuity at f168/185. The table added whose hair changes (the maroon-scarf pedestrian, a knit cap at frame 0 and longer locs at frame 191), masks appearing on bare faces partway through the take, and two walkers seen from behind entering at the right edge from about frame 120.
4. **Answer the guide's six questions, then choose three to five controls.** Write the stills prompt as five to ten decisive instructions. Append P41 to stills only, never to motion prompts. Where the shot carries no gold thread, as the SH020 job treated its frames, replace P41's last sentence, "Keep the specified mask and thread state.", with a sentence naming the mask state. `references/SH020_job_2026-10-30.md` holds the SH020 stills prompts as submitted; use them as a pattern, not as canon.
5. **Write the motion prompt to match its own endpoints.** Tested on three takes on the owned lane on 2026-10-31 (the section below); the rest of this step is still the method and not a measurement. Give one action and the camera behaviour. Name who leaves frame and who enters, and say that nobody appears or vanishes inside the frame. A prompt that says "no new people" while the closing frame holds people the opening frame does not invites a first-and-last-frame model to morph them in.
6. **Keep the locked camera.** The five Episode 1 street shots share one camera (Ruling 1 of 2026-10-30). SH020, SH070 and SH100 are locked off, with under 0.5 px of skyline shift inside each take (manifest, Measured Metrics). SH150's take is not: it pans from frame 0, about 89 px by f191, with a -25,+3 px skyline shift at f95, so its native last frame is off the locked framing. It needs a per-frame camera solve or a ruling from Tee before it serves as an end still; `tsws-repair-workflows` has the measurements. The SH150 Venetian prop composite FINAL is a different matter: conformed by width (1920x1071 with Void rows) it registers to the locked framing within 0.5 px on the static regions (measured 2026-10-31, `references/EP01_generation_cards_2026-10-31.md`), so that composite serves as an end still where the native frame does not; its fill scale does not, at up to 7 px off. SH135 is not regenerated either: its v10 still stays archived and byte-identical, and the Visual Bible's current repair status lists a separate v11 Venetian composite prepared under Tee's authorization on 2026-10-25. A street shot generated from text alone breaks continuity with its neighbours, so generate stills from the native frames and animate between them.
7. **Make the end frame first.** The end still is also the next shot's start frame. Pin anyone who also appears in the downstream approved frame to that frame's mask and hair. Then make the start still from the native first frame.
8. **Give the start still a head sheet, not the end still.** Crop the masked heads from the accepted end still onto a neutral grey sheet. Pass that as the second image, with one sentence saying it is a sheet of reference heads and not a scene; on SH020 the sentence was "The second image is a sheet of reference heads, not a scene.", placed straight after "Edit the first image only." This rests on one A/B pair, and that pair proved the layout fix only, not mask transfer: on SH020 the beige-coat pedestrian and the briefcase man came out in masks different from the sheet's (see the receipts and "Still unverified"). Check each shared person's mask against the end still before accepting the start still. Where one fails on a small face, a local warp can carry the end still's mask onto it with no paid run. On SH020 the carried masks read correctly at 1:1 and soft when zoomed, checked by eye only, and Tee accepted them on that basis (see the receipts). Put that or a paid retry to Tee on a card, with the softness written into the option's cost.
9. **Check every still before any video runs.** See "Checking a still" below. If one check fails, repair that defect without a paid run where you can. A corrective re-run is paid, so it waits for Tee's answer on a card, and it changes one variable at a time, as the realism guide's retest rule says.
10. **Check the clip, then hand it to Tee to watch.** See "Checking a clip" below.
11. **Save what was submitted.** Keep each submitted prompt verbatim, copied from the `run_model` call and not from a draft file, plus the prediction id, the output sha256 and the price. Commit them in this skill as `references/<SHOT>_job_<YYYY-MM-DD>.md`, in the form of `references/SH020_job_2026-10-30.md`, and link that file from the arc's dated `docs/devon/SYS_OPS_*` status doc when the arc closes. Ask Tee on a card whether to file the accepted stills and the head sheet on Drive beside the street masters, and record where they went. The session scratchpad and transcript do not survive the container. The Episode 1 run plan, one card per paid run with its inputs, prompt, acceptance check and quote, is `references/EP01_generation_cards_2026-10-31.md`.

## Checking a still

Run `.claude/skills/tsws-shot-prompt/scripts/align_still.py NATIVE.png EDITED.png OUT_PREFIX --boxes "name=x0,y0,x1,y1"` with the Python of a throwaway virtual environment in the session scratchpad (`pip install opencv-python-headless numpy`), never the project's own, because this repository does not pin OpenCV. It resizes the edit to native size, fits an affine, and prints the shift of three static regions before and after the fit. It writes the aligned still and a change map, then prints the shift of each person named in `--boxes`. It prints numbers only. For a still that is already native size and aligned, such as a delivered still, read its "plain resize" lines; the script fits a second affine to it, which moves the readings again. No pass or fail tolerance for a still's registration has been ruled. The 0.5 px skyline figure is a measurement of the native takes and the acceptance line for a motion take, not a limit for a still. Compare the numbers with the notes below, report the worst region with the estimator that read it, and put accept or reject to Tee on an `AskUserQuestion` card.

Its built-in regions were drawn on background that no person crosses in either SH020 native frame. They hold only for the street plate: the script refuses any other native size and prints which region set it used, and any shot that is not the locked street plate needs `--regions`, whatever its size. A region with a person in it measures the person. Keep each region on one surface too, because a region that spans surfaces the edit moved differently can average them away. The script reads each shift with phase correlation and then refines it with a translation ECC, because phase correlation alone pulled known sub-pixel shifts toward whole pixels by up to about 0.32 px. It keeps the ECC answer only within 1 px of its seed, because on a person who moved ECC can lock onto the background inside the box. The first version used phase correlation alone. On the raw end still it read 0.33 px at worst on the first regions and 0.56 px on the current ones. Read with ECC, both sets come to about 0.35 px on either axis (0.37 px as a vector on the lobby), so the gap between them was the estimator, not the people: the 0.33 reading was close to right, and on the lobby, where the edit changed the content, phase correlation over-read by about 0.2 px. On a generated edit the sub-pixel reading also moves with the estimator's smoothing: with an ECC prefilter of 1 to 11 px the delivered end still's skyline read 0.12 to 0.25 px and the start still's lobby +0.07 to -0.09 px. Treat differences under about 0.1 px as within the estimator's spread.

- **Size.** Nano Banana Pro edit at 2K, with `aspect_ratio` 16:9 requested, returned 2752x1536 on every run on 2026-10-30, about 0.8% wider than 16:9. A plain resize to 1920x1080 left the static regions 1.7 to 3.5 px off vertically. Read on the delivered files, the start still sat within about 0.1 px in all three regions, and the end still's skyline 0.18 px off vertically and its lobby 0.35 px (0.37 px as a vector), with the default 5 px prefilter, because the edit did not move every part of the frame together. Never stretch blindly. Align, measure every region, and report the worst one. These figures were read with OpenCV 5.0.0 and numpy 2.5.4; the script prints both versions, so a later re-run can tell version drift from a real difference.
- **People.** The end still's edit moved every body, by about 7 to 43 px: the beige-coat pedestrian about 7 to 8 px, the backpack man about 13, the briefcase man about 18 and the maroon-scarf pedestrian 30 to 43, mostly downward. On those bodies phase correlation answered only 0.08 to 0.47 against 0.83 to 0.91 on the static regions, and ECC disagreed with it by more than 1 px on every one, so the script printed the phase answer flagged. Read a body shift as rough and confirm its size another way, such as dense optical flow, before relying on it. Pasting new heads onto native bodies would then leave neck seams, so the aligned full render was kept. The start still's retry held all six people measured within 0.1 px (the boxes are in the job record) and was still kept as a full render, so that both stills came from one model and their textures match. Composite heads onto the native frame only when the bodies barely move and a texture mismatch with the other still does not matter; no threshold for "barely" has been measured. Record which you did and why.
- **Added and removed people.** Compare the edges of the frame as well as the subjects. The SH020 end-still edit dropped two walkers seen from behind and added a background man by the lobby. People with no visible face can be restored from native pixels along a minimum-difference seam, with no paid run. A seam repair restores everything on the native side of the seam, so check that strip person by person against the mask rules below, and look at whoever the seam crosses at full size: on SH020 the first seam cut a walker in two, and a re-cut along the edited person's edge brought him back whole (see the receipts). A static region inside the restored strip then measures native against native and reads zero, so read registration on the aligned render before the seam.
- **Mask canon, as of 2026-10-30.** The two mask bullets below restate the top section, headed APPROVED TEE RULING, of `TSWS_Auren_Vespera_Character_Sheets_MASTER_v1.4_2026-10-25.md`, and Ruling 4 of 2026-10-30. They are a snapshot, not canon. Before checking a still, reread both files and search `00_CANON` for a later ruling. Where a later ruling differs from these bullets, the ruling governs, and the bullets are out of date until revised.
- **Masks before the fall** (SH020, SH070 and the start of SH100). Every visible street face wears a physically fitted Venetian-style eye and nose half-mask, each with a stable identity, silhouette, material, color, ornament and attachment, and no superseded design remains: no medical masks, skin-shell faces, feathers, headpieces, horns or Anahata decoration. Background masks belong to one restrained Venetian family, and the approved Auren and Vespera pair is not indiscriminately cloned across the crowd; the canon does not require every mask to be unique. A pedestrian whose mask a dated ruling names wears exactly that mask; on 2026-10-30 that was the maroon-scarf pedestrian's mottled navy half-mask with gold flecks and a gold rim (Ruling 4). Each person who keeps a mask across two stills wears the same one in both. At 4x zoom, a small dark eye mask can read as sunglasses; flag it rather than pass it.
- **Masks from the fall on.** In SH100 the same worn mask detaches intact and falls, and the face beneath stays intact, with no hand removal, melting or face substitution. From then on the street crowd is unmasked, and both leads keep their masks through Episode 1's closing return.
- **Belongings.** The case carrier keeps his briefcase in his anatomical right hand in every street still and take; the 2026-10-25 ruling makes that continuity mandatory in SH100.
- **Realism gate.** Reject or mark pending on skin smoothing, anatomy drift, invented or sterile environment detail, implausible reflections or shadows, staged gaze, changing proportions, or mask or mouth instability.

## Checking a clip

These checks come from the clip acceptance list of the SH020 job of 2026-10-30. On 2026-10-31 the first and last frame checks and the drift scan ran on three real takes on the owned lane (the section below and the job record), the by-eye checks ran on contact sheets and crops, and continuous playback has not been judged by anyone.

- Get the take here and record its sha256, size, duration, frame rate and whether it has audio.
- Extract the first, middle and last frames. Run `align_still.py` on each against the native frame and read its "plain resize" lines, which come before any fit and measure the camera; the "after affine alignment" lines remove a camera shift by construction and cannot show drift. For a locked shot, the skyline registration matches the native master within 0.5 px, the bound the manifest measured inside SH020, SH070 and SH100. The script was written for stills, and this use of it is unverified.
- Check that the first and last frames match the two accepted stills.
- Every person keeps one mask and one hairstyle on every frame, and nobody appears or vanishes inside the frame. Recheck the times where the manifest logged defects; for SH020 those are the foreground masks at 0, 3.0 and 3.5 s, the beige-coat face at 5.0, 6.0 and 7.0 s and the hair and mask edges at 7.0 and 7.7 s.
- Apply the realism gate as for a still, and record each defect with its time.
- If a take is almost right, keep both stills and the prompt, change one variable only, and record the change.
- A take is not accepted until Tee has watched it end to end. Passing the checks above only shows they ran. Report the take as pending his viewing until he says otherwise.

- **Handing a Cinema Studio run to Tee.** Nothing here can press Generate in Cinema Studio, so the run is his, in the `paste-loop` form. - **First and last frames.** The Kling 4.0 API (`kwaivgi/kling-v4.0/image-to-video`) takes `image`, `last_image` and up to nine middle `keyframes` (image, last_image and keyframes together at most 10), a whole number `duration` from 3 to 30 s, 720p or 1080p, and no seed (`get_model_schema`, 2026-10-31). Whether the Cinema Studio composer shows a last-frame slot was unverified on 2026-10-30. If it does not, run the same prompt on the model page or through the connector.
- **Audio and frame rate.** Set audio off where the tool allows and strip any that comes back; the street native masters are silent. The EP01 working cut is a 30 fps timeline while the street masters run at 24 fps (`TSWS_EP01_OPEN_GAPS.md`, PIC-06), so record each take's frame rate. - **Never resubmit a run that timed out on the client.** The run keeps going on the server. Poll it with `get_prediction` and a `wait_seconds` under 60. The schema allows 100, but a call with 100 on 2026-10-30 came back "timed out after 60s" from the client.
