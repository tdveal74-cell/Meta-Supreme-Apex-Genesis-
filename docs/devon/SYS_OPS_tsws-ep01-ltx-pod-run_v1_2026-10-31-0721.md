Drift is the new `drift_scan.py` over the mp4, every fourth frame, by phase correlation alone; `align_still.py` refines the same seed with an ECC fit, and the two read one frame up to about 0.17 px apart (an estimator difference a verifier reproduced), so the scan is the overview and `align_still` on the lossless frames is the measurement.

## What the chain taught, in one place

The one guide-only segment that stayed sane for its first five seconds held the camera at 0.07 px against 0.21 and 0.34 on the still-anchored takes, and the next guide-only segment drifted 0.67 px, so there is no rule about guide-only camera hold, one sample each way. The nine frame guide did carry motion across the seam both times (mean difference 3.4 to 4.9 of 255 against 8.1 for ordinary motion eight frames apart), and both guide-only runs hallucinated in the same place; one seed is one sample, and two seeds in one shape is a pattern worth one more test, which is what he ruled. The still-anchored first frame sits 0.06 to 0.33 px from the frames after it, which is Tee's to weigh and is below anything a viewer sees. And the A5 card itself carries a conflict the chain exposed: its prompt says the grey-coat woman "stands still where she stopped", while the SH150 end still it must land on has her at mid distance, walking, and has the maroon-scarf woman gone. The stills win over the prompt in a locked-camera chain, so the generated middle follows the stills; whether the SH150 composite or the standing rule is canon for the end of the shot is a ruling, and it goes on a card.
